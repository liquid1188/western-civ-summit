    title: "Natural Law and the American Founding: Rights, Religious Freedom, and Constitutional Government"
    description: Beginning with the Declaration of Independence’s appeal to natural law and the Constitution’s framework of limited government, this session will examine how inherent rights inform constitutional government. What responsibilities follow for legislators, courts, and citizens? How can this inheritance contribute to contemporary international discussion?
  - time: "3:20–4:00 p.m."
    label: Session II
    title: "Human Dignity Under Threat: Religious Persecution and Human Trafficking"
    description: Beginning with the experience of persecuted Christian communities in Nigeria and Nicaragua, this session will examine the protection of life, religious practice, and conscience. It will also address human trafficking as a grave violation of personal dignity, along with illegal mass migration and its aftermath, and will identify practical avenues for diplomatic, legislative, and civil society action.
  - time: "4:00–4:40 p.m."
    label: Session III
    title: "Principles in Practice: Freedom and Human Dignity in Latin America and Beyond"
    description: Public leaders will discuss concrete efforts to protect human life, strengthen the family, defend religious freedom, and uphold the rule of law. Drawing on Latin American experience, including Chile, the session will consider resistance to Communist and other authoritarian ideologies and the responsibilities that accompany electoral success. The discussion will examine achievements, obstacles, and lessons that may assist leaders in other countries.
